The system

Four phases, in order

Every phase sets up the next one. Ads go out last, after there's a website and a busy-looking page for people to land on.

  1. 01

    Build the website

    A fast, mobile-first site that answers what people want to know: what you do, where you work, what your work looks like, and how to reach you.

    • Home page with services, service area and real job photos
    • Tap-to-call phone button and a quote request form
    • SEO setup: page titles, Google-friendly structure, sitemap
    • Blog posts that answer the questions customers actually search
    • Analytics, so we can see which ads send visitors who call
    • Your own domain name (yourbusiness.com)

    About 1–2 weeks

  2. 02

    Create the social pages

    Facebook and Instagram business pages that match the website, so the brand looks the same everywhere someone finds you.

    • Facebook business page and Instagram business account
    • Profile photo, cover image, bio and contact buttons
    • Website link and phone number on every profile
    • Reviews turned on, plus a plan for collecting them
    • Meta Business Suite and ads account, owned by you
    • Google Business Profile, so you show up on Google Maps and local search

    About 1 week, alongside phase 1

  3. 03

    Prime the pages with posts

    Nobody trusts an empty page. Before any ad runs, the pages fill up with real content, so new visitors see an active business with a history of good work.

    • A month's worth of posts, written and designed ahead of time
    • A mix of before-and-afters, finished jobs, tips, the people behind the business, and reviews
    • Story-style long posts like the Teton Angel launch posts
    • A posting calendar so the pages never go quiet
    • Captions, hashtags and local tags written for each post

    About 2–4 weeks of posting before ads start

  4. 04

    Run the ad campaigns

    On Facebook and Instagram, the posts that are already doing well get boosted, then rotated as ads. On Google, ads show up when someone searches for exactly what you do. Either way, the weak ads get cut each week and the budget moves to whatever is bringing in calls. See exactly how below.

    • Facebook and Instagram ads targeted by location, radius and audience
    • Optional Google search ads on the keywords your customers type
    • Several ad versions running at once, tested against each other
    • Weekly cuts: losers off, winners get more budget
    • Fresh ads rotated in so the audience doesn't get bored
    • A plain-English monthly report covering every channel

    Ongoing, month to month

Blog posts for SEO and conversion

Posts that get found, then get calls

Ads stop when the budget stops. A good blog post keeps working: Google sends people to it for years, it turns into a week of social posts, and every one ends by asking the reader to take the next step.

No ad budget needed. Visitors who find a blog post on Google cost nothing per click, and they're already looking for an answer, which makes them some of the best leads you'll get. Ads speed things up. The blog keeps working after they stop.
1SearchSomeone searches a question your customers ask. Your post answers it.
2SocialThe same post becomes a long story post and a few shorter posts on Facebook and Instagram.
3SiteEvery version links back to the website, where they can see more of your work.
4ConvertThe post ends with one clear action: call, request a quote, or buy. Every click is tracked.

What goes into every post

  • A title people actually search for, written around one question or topic
  • A meta description that makes the Google result worth clicking
  • A real story or a real answer, not filler written for robots
  • Real photos with descriptive alt text
  • Local places and details, so you show up for searches near you
  • Links to your other pages, so readers keep going
  • Structured data that tells Google who wrote it and what it's about
  • One call to action at the end, with click tracking on it

What this looks like for a local service business

Same approach, different questions. A pool company might write:

How long does pool plaster last? 5 signs it's time to replaster Plaster vs. pebble finish: what's the difference? What to expect on replaster day Before & after: a backyard pool in [your town]

Each one answers a real search, shows real jobs, and ends with "Call for a free quote."

Adding Google Ads

Two kinds of ads, two different jobs

Social ads put your work in front of people who weren't looking for you yet. Google ads catch people at the moment they search for what you do. Running both covers both, but it costs more.

Facebook & Instagram

Get discovered

  • Shows your photos and story to local people while they scroll
  • Cheap reach: thousands of people for about $10 a day
  • Builds familiarity, so they think of you when the need comes up
  • Best for work that looks good in pictures

Google search ads

Get chosen

  • Shows up at the top when someone searches "[your service] near me"
  • Fewer people, but they're ready to hire now
  • You pay per click, and clicks usually cost more than on social
  • Includes call ads, so people can call you straight from the search results

Questions

FAQ

Do I have to post anything myself?

No. You send photos from your jobs when you have them, and I turn them into posts. The more real photos you send, the better the ads do.

Why not just boost a post and call it done?

One boosted post is one guess. Running several ads and cutting the losers each week is how $10 a day turns into calls instead of likes.

Who owns the pages and accounts?

You do. The website domain, the Facebook page, the Instagram account and the ad account are all in your name. I get access to manage them.

Do I need Google Ads?

Not to start. The blog and your Google Business Profile bring in search traffic for free, and social ads are the cheaper way to get noticed. Google ads make sense once you want more calls from people searching right now and you're OK with a bigger daily budget.

Can I change the ad budget?

Yes, any time. $10 a day is a good place to start. When something is clearly working, more budget gets you more of it.

How soon will the phone ring?

Ads usually start after a few weeks of posting, and the first two weeks of ads are for testing. Most of the learning happens in month one, and month two is when the budget is focused on what works.

Will you use AI-generated photos of my work?

No. Customers want to see your actual work, so before-and-after photos from real jobs carry the campaign. Design and graphics go around those photos.
